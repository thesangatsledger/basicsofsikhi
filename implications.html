What it means

The numbers, and what they tell you

The report shows the numbers. This page says what they mean. It also says what they do not mean. Both matter.

Read this first. We are not Basics of Sikhi. We are not Everythings 13. We have no tie to them, and they did not make or approve this page.

This page explains public records. It is our reading of them. It is not legal advice and it is not a finding by any court or regulator. A strange number on a form is a reason to ask a question. It is not proof that anyone did wrong. Nobody named or described here has been charged with anything. Where we compare to WE Charity, we compare the warning signs, not the wrongdoing. Nothing here says the people behind Basics of Sikhi did what was alleged about WE.

What it means, in one minute

You can give to these charities, but you cannot see where most of the money goes. The charities can see it. They have not shown it.

That is the whole problem. It is not a crime to be unclear. But a charity runs on trust, and trust needs light.

You give $1, to keep Jagraj Singh's work going. Through CanadaHelps, it goes to the charity in Canada, Basics of Sikhi Society. In 2024, 95 cents of it went to consultants the charity does not name (CRA line 4860). How much of it went to the programs, the teaching, videos and outreach? The charity stopped saying in 2021. In three years, the consultants' share went from 7 cents to 95 cents of every dollar. In the US, the sister charity's wages in 2025 ($130,794) were more than everything donors gave ($120,559). People asked about this online. The posts were deleted, some within minutes. So ask: where does the money go? This is not a claim that anyone committed a crime. It is a fair question.

What it means, and what it does not

What the records do show

  • Most of the Canadian money goes to people the public is not told about.
  • That share rose from 7% to 95% in three years.
  • The charity stopped saying how much reached its programs.
  • The US arm paid more in wages in 2025 than donors gave.
  • Several forms have answers that cannot all be true.

What the records do not show

  • They do not show theft.
  • They do not show who the consultants are. They may have done honest work.
  • They do not show money moving between the three charities.
  • They do not show that any person broke a law.
  • They do not show what the charities would say if asked.

Why it matters to you

If you give. You have a right to know where your money goes. Right now, for most of the Canadian money, you cannot.

If you watch the videos. The work you see is real. The question is not whether videos get made. It is what share of your gift pays for them, and who decides.

For the Sangat. Dasvandh is a duty. A duty given in trust should be easy to follow. When it is not, everyone loses: the donors, the honest workers, and the name of the man who started it.

The US charity

In the US, the charity's officers list their pay as $0. Yet in 2025 the wage bill was bigger than all the money donors gave. The savings paid the rest. The public cannot see who got the wages. That is legal at these amounts. It is also exactly the kind of gap donors should ask about.

What happened with WE Charity

WE Charity was a Canadian charity with a famous name. It also had a business, ME to WE, that used the same brand. For years, schools and families gave to it without asking hard questions.

In 2020, the federal government picked WE to run a large student grant program. Reporters then found that WE had paid members of the Prime Minister's family for speaking events. Parliament and the Ethics Commissioner looked into it. Reporters also found it hard to tell where the charity ended and the business began. WE pulled out of the program and soon wound down its work in Canada.

The lesson Canadians took from WE was simple. A good name is not the same as a clear record. Donors should ask early, not late.

What would fix this

The charities can clear this up. It would take a few pages:

  1. Name the consultants, or at least say what work they did and whether any are tied to the board.
  2. Fill in the program line on the Canadian returns, and correct past ones.
  3. Fix the US "government grants" entry, or name the government.
  4. Publish one simple yearly report that covers all three charities together.

If they do, we will publish it here, in full, at the top of the report.

What you can do

  • Ask. Email the charity. Ask question 1 and question 3 from the report. Keep it polite and in writing.
  • Check. Every number has a source link. Look for yourself.
  • Choose. If you want to see your gift at work, give where you can see it, and ask every charity the same questions.
  • Be fair. Do not attack people. Ask about the money.
