From 7% to 95%: where Basics of Sikhi's money went

People gave money to Basics of Sikhi. They gave to spread Sikhi. The charity's own records show where the money went. Most of it went to people the records do not name.

You give $1, to keep Jagraj Singh's work going. Through CanadaHelps, it goes to the charity in Canada, Basics of Sikhi Society. In 2024, 95 cents of it went to consultants the charity does not name (CRA line 4860). How much reached the programs, the teaching, videos and outreach? The charity stopped saying in 2021. In three years, the consultants' share went from 7 cents to 95 cents of every dollar. In the US, the sister charity's wages in 2025 ($130,794) were more than everything donors gave ($120,559). People asked about this online. The posts were deleted, some within minutes. So ask: where does the money go? This is not a claim that anyone committed a crime. It is a fair question.

The story

Jagraj Singh started Basics of Sikhi. He walked up to strangers. He talked to them about Sikhi. He asked for nothing. People loved him for it.

He died in 2017. People still give money in his name.

That money goes to charities in three countries. In Canada, it is Basics of Sikhi Society. In the UK and the US, it is Everythings 13. The US charity calls Basics of Sikhi "our main project."IRS

Every charity must tell the government how it spends money. Those reports are public. Anyone can read them. Few people do. We did.

Canada: the consultants

A consultant is an outside worker. A charity pays them for a job. A charity does not have to say who they are.

From the charity's T3010 returns: total spent is line 5100, consultants line 4860, staff line 4880, programs line 5000. "Not said" means the charity left the program line blank. The 2025 return is not public yet.

Read the table from top to bottom.

In 2021, consultants got 7 cents of each dollar. One year later they got 79 cents. Then 88. Then 95.

Staff pay went the other way. It fell from C$23,881 to C$5,022. Then the staff pay line went blank.

In 2023, the consultants got exactly C$96,000. That is C$8,000 a month, every month. That looks like a wage. But no one is named.

Now look at the last column. Until 2020, the charity said how much went to its programs. Programs are the real work: teaching, videos, outreach. In 2021 it stopped saying. That was the year the consultant money began. It has not said since.

We do not say nothing went to programs. We say the charity stopped telling anyone.

Whose money was it?

It was yours. Most gifts came through CanadaHelps, the giving website. CanadaHelps takes gifts from regular people and passes them on. It has passed C$394,461 to this charity over the years. In 2024, it and sites like it sent C$129,221 of the C$156,066 the charity took in.CRA

So this is the Sangat's money. In 2024, 95 cents of each dollar spent went to people the Sangat is not told about.

Government money, no staff

In 2021, the charity got C$40,306 from the federal government. In 2022 it got C$20,971. In 2023, C$4,601.CRA In those years, most such money helped pay workers' wages. But in 2021, the charity said it paid its staff nothing.

The answer that changed

The form asks a question. Are the board members tied to each other? Are they family, or close in business?

In 2018, the charity said yes. All five were tied. Every year after, the same people said no.CRA

This matters. If most of the board is tied together, the rules are different. The charity counts as a private foundation instead. Both answers cannot be true. Only the charity knows which is wrong.

2025. Donors gave $120,559. The charity paid $130,794 in wages. It paid out more in wages than it took in from every donor that year. It made up the gap from savings. The savings fell from $190,174 to $119,348 in one year.IRS

The officers say they were paid nothing. So who got the wages? The return does not say. It only has to name people paid over $100,000.

Two years earlier, wages were $16,737. They grew about eight times over.

2024. There was one worker. That worker got $75,000. The return does not give a name.IRS The law allows this. The law does not stop donors from asking.

The "government grants." In 2024 the charity took in $230,176. It wrote down $230,172 of it as money from the government. Only $4 came from anyone else.IRS No government is named. There is one common reason a charity gets money like this: a pandemic tax refund tied to wages. But this charity paid only $7,247 in wages in 2021. That is far too small for a refund this big. Either the form is wrong, or there is government money no one can see.

Money sent overseas. In 2024, $7,684 went abroad as grants. The part of the form that must name who got it is empty. Another part says $7,555 paid for "video making" in India, by three people.IRS Who were they?

All of it is "program." The 2024 form says every dollar went to programs. Nothing to running the office. Nothing to fundraising. But $8,552 of it paid accountants.IRS The tax rules say that is office cost, not program. This makes the charity look better than its own numbers show.

The tax number that does not exist

Dasvandh Network is a Sikh giving website in the US. In 2023 it said it sent $39,580 to "EVERYTHINGS13-BASICS OF SIKHI."IRS

It gave a tax number for the charity: 82-5173426. That number is not on the US government's list of charities.IRS The real number for Everythings 13 is 32-0647672.

That $39,580 was half of everything Everythings 13 said it got from donors that year.IRS With the wrong number, the government cannot match the gift to the charity's books.

The UK charity has 12 to 14 staff on its payroll. So a payroll can be done. The Canadian charity pays nearly everything to consultants instead.

What we do not know

We want to be fair. So here is what the records do not show.

  • They do not name the consultants or the US worker. Those people may have done honest work.
  • They do not show money moving between the three charities.
  • Paying teachers and video makers as consultants is common. It is legal.
  • Some of this may be mistakes on forms. But mistakes on forms sent to three governments are a problem too.

None of this proves anyone did wrong. We do not claim it does. It does prove the public cannot see where the money went. Only the charities can fix that.

The ten questions

  1. Who got the C$97,021 for consulting in 2024? Who got the C$96,000 in 2023? What work did they do?
  2. Are any of those consultants on the board? Are any family of the board? Are any also paid by the UK or US charity?
  3. Why did the Canadian charity stop saying what it spent on programs in 2021?
  4. In 2018 the board said they were tied together. Every year after, they said they were not. Which is true?
  5. What was the C$40,306 from the government in 2021, a year with no staff pay?
  6. Who was the one US worker paid $75,000 in 2024? Are they family of an officer?
  7. Which government gave Everythings 13 US $230,172 in 2024? If none did, when will the form be fixed?
  8. The officers say they were paid nothing. So who got the $130,794 in wages in 2025?
  9. Who in India was paid $7,555 to make videos in 2024?
  10. Why was the $39,580 from Dasvandh Network sent under a tax number that does not exist?
If the money went where donors think it went, each question has a short answer. Asking where the money goes is not an insult to Sikhi. It is not an insult to Jagraj Singh. He stood for the truth.

We asked. They deleted it.

We asked these questions on Reddit first. The posts on r/Sikh and r/Sikhpolitics were taken down. One comment quoting these same numbers was gone within minutes. None of them named a person. None of them made a charge. No one from the charities has answered.

Their side

Basics of Sikhi Society, Everythings 13 in the UK and US, and Dasvandh Network may answer any question here. We will post their answer in full, at the top of this page, with no edits. If we got something wrong, tell us and show us the record. We will fix it. Write to sangatledger@gmail.com.

Check it yourself

Do not take our word for it. Each number takes about five minutes to check. It is free.

  1. Canada. Open the CRA page for Basics of Sikhi Society. Pick a year. Find line 4860 (consultants) and line 5100 (total spent).
  2. US. Open ProPublica for Everythings 13. Open the 2025 return. Find line 12 (wages). Compare it with the money donated.
  3. UK. Open the Charity Commission page for charity 1143262. The money page lists every year.
  4. Ask. Anyone can ask a US charity for its last three tax returns. By law, it must send them within 30 days. If you give, ask in writing. Share what you get back.
