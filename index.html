From 7% to 95%: where Basics of Sikhi's money went

In three years, the Canadian charity behind Basics of Sikhi went from spending 7% of its money on unnamed consultants to 95%. In the same years it stopped reporting what it spent on its charitable programs. In the US, its sister charity spent more on wages in 2025 than it raised from every donor, while its officers list their pay as $0.

Basics of Sikhi was built by the late Jagraj Singh, who walked up to strangers and talked to them about Sikhi. People still donate in his memory. The brand now runs through charities in three countries: Basics of Sikhi Society in Canada, and two charities named Everythings 13 in the UK and the US. The US charity's own tax return calls Basics of Sikhi "our main project."IRS

All three file their finances with the government, and anyone can read them for free. This is what they say.

Four things stand out.

The consultants arrived all at once. Consulting fees went from C$5,619 in 2021 to C$91,200 in 2022, a sixteen-fold jump in one year. They have been above 79% of spending every year since.

Staff pay went the other way. As consulting rose, reported staff pay fell from C$23,881 (2022) to C$5,022 (2023) to none on line 4880 (2024). The 2024 return shows C$5,258 on line 4891, which it does not explain.

The numbers are round. The 2023 consulting figure is exactly C$96,000, or C$8,000 a month. Fixed monthly payments look like a retainer or salary, not occasional contract work.

The program numbers stopped when the consultants started. From 2018 to 2020 the charity reported how much it spent on its charitable programs. From 2021, the year the money started moving to consultants, it stopped. Four returns in a row leave the program line blank. This page does not claim nothing reached programs. It does say the charity's own filings no longer show how much did.

Where it came from: you

Almost all of this money came from ordinary donors. Most Canadian gifts reach the charity through CanadaHelps, the online giving platform, which passes on individual donations and issues the tax receipts. CanadaHelps has passed C$394,461 to Basics of Sikhi Society over the years on record. In 2024, C$129,221 of the charity's C$156,066 in revenue arrived as gifts through platforms like this.CRA

In other words, this is the Sangat's money. Ninety-five cents of each dollar spent in 2024 went to people the Sangat is not told about.

Government money, no payroll

In 2021 the charity reported C$40,306 in federal government revenue, then C$20,971 in 2022 and C$4,601 in 2023 (line 4540).CRA Pandemic wage subsidies and Canada Summer Jobs are the usual sources for small charities in those years, and both pay toward wages. In 2021 the charity reported C$0 in staff pay.

The arm's-length answer that flipped

The T3010 asks whether directors deal with each other at arm's length, meaning they are not related or otherwise tied to each other. On the 2018 return, all five directors were marked not at arm's length. On every return since, the same people are marked at arm's length, with no change in the board to explain it.CRA This matters. A charitable organization must have more than half its directors at arm's length. If it doesn't, CRA treats it as a private foundation, which has different rules. One of those answers is wrong, and only the charity can say which.

2025: wages ate more than every donation. Wages and benefits came to $130,794. Everything donated that year came to $120,559. The charity covered the gap from savings, which fell from $190,174 to $119,348 in one year. The officers list their pay as $0.IRS Wages grew eightfold in two years ($16,737 to $130,794), and the return names no employee, because it only has to name those paid over $100,000.

2024: one employee, $75,000, no name. The 2024 return reports exactly one W-2 employee and $75,000 in salary (plus $6,105 in payroll tax). Every officer is listed at $0. The law lets a charity leave an employee at that pay level unnamed. It does not stop donors asking who it is, and whether they are related to anyone on the board.IRS

2024: almost all of it recorded as "government grants." Of $230,176 raised in 2024, $230,172 is entered on the line for government grants (Part VIII, line 1e). Other contributions: $4. No government payer is named.IRS The usual legitimate explanation, a pandemic Employee Retention Credit refund, does not fit. The charity's 2021 wages were $7,247, far too little to produce a credit this size. Either the return is wrong or there is a government grant the public cannot see.

Money sent abroad, recipients not named. The 2024 return reports $7,684 in grants to foreign organizations or individuals. The schedule that must name foreign grant recipients is empty. Another schedule lists $7,555 for "video making" in India by three people, and $129 for "education articles" in the UK.IRS

Everything is "program." The 2024 return books 100% of spending as program work, with $0 for management and $0 for fundraising. That includes $8,552 in accounting fees, which IRS instructions class as management.IRS The result makes the charity look more efficient on paper than its own figures support.

The donation that went to a tax ID that doesn't exist

Dasvandh Network, a US Sikh giving platform, reported paying $39,580 in 2023 to "EVERYTHINGS13-BASICS OF SIKHI" under tax ID 82-5173426.IRS That number is not in the IRS master file of tax-exempt organizations.IRS Everythings 13's real tax ID is 32-0647672. The $39,580 equals 51% of all contributions Everythings 13 reported for that year.IRS Because the ID is wrong, the IRS cannot match the grant to the charity's books. Whose error it is is a question for both organizations.

Two people. The register lists three trustee entries, but the same name appears twice under two different trustee IDs, once as chair since 2013 and once as an ordinary trustee since 2022. That may be a duplicate record. If so, a charity with £623,030 a year in income is governed by two distinct people. The Charity Commission recommends at least three.CCEW

It has a payroll. The UK arm reports 12 to 14 staff. The Canadian arm reports almost none and pays nearly everything to unnamed consultants. "Too small for a payroll" doesn't explain a structure that already exists one country over.

What the filings do not show

To be fair to the organizations:

  • No filing names the Canadian consultants or the US employee. They may be entirely legitimate.
  • No filing shows money moving between the US, UK and Canadian charities. Shared costs, such as video production, could be paid by one arm for another without showing up as a grant.
  • Paying teachers and video staff as contractors is common and legal.
  • Several anomalies above could be form-filling errors. Errors on returns filed with three governments are a problem in their own right.

None of this is proof of wrongdoing, and this page does not claim it is. It is proof that the public record does not answer the questions a donor would ask.

The questions

  1. Who received the C$97,021 in consulting fees in 2024, and the C$96,000 in 2023? What work did they do?
  2. Is any of those consultants a director, a director's relative, or a company connected to one? Are any also paid by the UK or US charity?
  3. Why did the Canadian charity stop reporting its program spending in 2021, the year consulting fees took off?
  4. Which is true: the 2018 answer that the directors are not at arm's length, or every later answer that they are?
  5. What was the C$40,306 in federal government revenue in 2021, in a year with no staff pay?
  6. Who is the one US employee paid $75,000 in 2024? Are they related to an officer?
  7. What government paid Everythings 13 US $230,172 in 2024? If none, when will the return be corrected?
  8. How does a charity whose officers list $0 pay end up spending more on wages than it raised, as Everythings 13 US did in 2025?
  9. Who in India was paid $7,555 for video making in 2024?
  10. Why did Dasvandh Network report a $39,580 grant under a tax ID that does not exist?
Every one of these has a simple answer if the money went where donors believe it went. Asking where the money goes is not disrespect to the faith, or to the memory of the man who built this.

Asked, and removed

These questions were first raised in Sikh community forums on Reddit. Posts on r/Sikh and r/Sikhpolitics were removed. A comment citing the same CRA figures on an existing discussion thread was removed by a moderator within minutes. None of those posts named an individual or made an allegation. No one from any of the organizations has responded publicly.

Check it yourself

You do not need to trust this page. Every figure takes about five minutes to check, for free.

  1. Canada. Open the CRA listing for Basics of Sikhi Society. Open any year and find line 4860 (consultants), line 4880 (staff pay), line 5000 (programs) and line 5100 (total spent).
  2. United States. Open ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer for Everythings 13. In the 2024 return, see Part VIII line 1e (government grants) and Part IX line 7 (other salaries). In the 2025 return, see line 12 (salaries).
  3. United Kingdom. Open the Charity Commission register for charity 1143262. The financial history page has every year. The full accounts, which list grants and related-party payments, are free PDFs.
  4. Ask. Anyone can ask a US charity for its last three returns and its application for tax exemption. By law it must provide them within 30 days. If you donate, ask in writing, and share what you get back.
