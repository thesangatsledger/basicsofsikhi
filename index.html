95 cents of every dollar, to consultants nobody will name

Basics of Sikhi Society's own filings with the Canada Revenue Agency show where the money goes. Almost all of it goes to one line: unnamed professional and consulting fees. For four straight years, the charity did not report how much went to its charitable programs.

Published October 31, 2026 · Sources: CRA T3010 returns, IRS Form 990 filings, Charity Commission for England and Wales register · Corrections log
94.8%of Basics of Sikhi Society's 2024 spending went to "professional and consulting fees." No consultant is named.

The trend

Basics of Sikhi Society (CRA registration 752991695RR0001) is the Canadian registered charity behind the Basics of Sikhi name. It fundraises from the Sangat, including donors who give in memory of its founder, the late Jagraj Singh. Here is how the share of its spending going to "professional and consulting fees" (T3010 line 4860) has moved:

Total spending fell from 2022 to 2024. The consulting share rose every year. By 2024, C$97,021 of C$102,334 went out under one line, about C$8,085 a month. The return does not say who received it or what work it paid for.

Four years without a program figure

Each year, a registered charity is asked on the T3010 to divide its total spending among charitable programs (line 5000), management and administration (5010), fundraising (5020), gifts to qualified donees (5050) and other spending (5040). These figures are how donors and the CRA see how much of the money reached the charitable purpose.

For 2021, 2022 and 2023, Basics of Sikhi Society reported total spending but left every one of those lines blank. For 2024, the split is blank again. The 2018 and 2019 returns did fill in the split and it adds up, so the charity has completed this section before.

A blank is not the same as zero, and this page does not claim that nothing went to programs. It is a fact that for four straight years, the charity's public filings do not say how much did.

The money coming in

In 2024 Basics of Sikhi Society reported revenue of C$156,066. Of that, C$129,221 (about 83%) was gifts from other registered charities. Most came from a single Canadian registered charity, BN 896568417RR0001, which reported gifts of about C$108,000, C$108,000 and C$117,000 in its fiscal years ending June 2022, 2023 and 2024.

So most of the money arrives from another charity, and almost all of it leaves as fees to consultants who are not named.

Who sits on both sides of the table

The T3010 asks whether directors are at arm's length from each other. On the 2018 return, the directors were listed as not at arm's length. On the 2019 return, the same directors were listed as at arm's length. The board went to four names in 2020, and a new trustee was added in mid-2022.

Charity-sector practitioners say the place to look in a filing like this is related-party disclosure. It is legal for a charity to route its spending through contractors or a management company. What regulators care about is whether money goes to parties connected to the directors, and whether that is disclosed. We have not found a management-services agreement or a related-party disclosure in the Canadian filings.

The same name in three countries

The Basics of Sikhi brand also runs through two entities named Everythings 13:

  • United Kingdom: Everythings 13 (registered charity 1143262). Its most recent filing on the Charity Commission register reports income of £623,030 and 12 employees. It runs a payroll.
  • United States: Everythings 13 (EIN 32-0647672). On its 2025 Form 990, wages came to 108% of donations, more than it raised from donors. On its 2024 return, US$230,172 of US$230,176 in revenue was coded as government grants, with no explanation.

The UK arm runs a payroll with 12 staff. The Canadian arm reports salaries as blank and sends almost all its money to unnamed consultants. "Too small for payroll" doesn't explain a structure that exists one country over.

The questions

None of this proves wrongdoing, and this page does not claim any. The filings do raise questions that only the charity can answer:

  1. Who received the C$97,021 in professional and consulting fees in 2024, and what services did they provide?
  2. Is any of those consultants a director, a relative of a director, or a company connected to one?
  3. Is there a management-services agreement, and with whom?
  4. Why was the program, administration and fundraising split left blank for 2021 through 2024?
  5. What is BN 896568417RR0001, who directs it, and why does it fund most of this charity's revenue?
  6. What changed between the 2018 and 2019 returns to move the same directors from "not at arm's length" to "at arm's length"?
  7. What does line 4891 (C$5,258 in 2024) pay for?
Every question above has a simple answer if the money went where donors believe it went. Asking where the money goes is not disrespect to the faith or to the memory of the man who built this.

Asked, and removed

These figures were first raised in Sikh community forums on Reddit. The posts on r/Sikh and r/Sikhpolitics were removed. A comment citing the same CRA figures on an existing discussion thread was removed by a moderator within minutes. None of those posts named an individual or made an allegation. No one from the organization has responded publicly.
